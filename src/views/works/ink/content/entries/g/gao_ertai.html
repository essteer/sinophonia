Gao Ertai 高爾泰

Gao Ertai 高爾泰1935 is an artist and writer born in Nanjing, Jiangsu, known for his writing on aesthetics and his memoir In Search of My Homeland 尋找家園, an account of life on the receiving end of waves of brutal political campaigns in the decades following the communist victory in China, up until his eventual exile in the United States in the early 1990s.

Gao's prodigal talent as a painter gained him entry to a prestigious academy at a young age, and by his early 20s he was teaching fine arts. His essay On Aesthetics 論美 caused a national stir upon publication in a prominent Beijing magazine in early 1957, and though it earned him supporters who would provide a lifeline on more than one occasion, it also secured for him the ire of the authorities.

When the Anti-Rightist Campaign 反右運動1957-1959 broke out later that year, Gao was labelled a Rightist 右派 and sent to the notorious Jiabiangou 夾邊溝 labour camp, at which an estimated 2,500 of 3,000 inmates died of starvation during the period.

He survived that ordeal, but his identity was fixed as an enemy of the state. With each subsequent stage of political upheaval he was one of the many routinely targeted for hard labour, struggle sessions, and various forms of privation and ostracisation.

The events he recounts are well known today to those familiar with China's recent history, but it is not the first-hand nature of his account that makes his memoir compelling. This is a remarkable work for the sense it gives of someone at peace with himself, who has come to terms with the injustices he has faced as well as his own faults and regrets.

Gao relates his past with dignity and the humble awareness that for all of the trauma and pain he has endured, he is not unique for having lived through this. He shows as much interest in the humanity of those around him as his own, remembering those who didn't make it, those whose actions were vital to his own survival, and even those complicit in what took place.
