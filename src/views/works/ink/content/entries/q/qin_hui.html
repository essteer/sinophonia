Qin Hui 秦暉

Qin Hui 秦暉1953 is a former professor of history at Tsinghua University 清華大學 whose studies have incorporated ancient and modern Chinese history, social movements and political upheavals. He is currently a faculty member at the Chinese University of Hong Kong 香港中文大學.

His book Moving Away from the Imperial Regime走出帝制 was conceived as a short series of articles to coincide with the 100th anniversary of the Xinhai Revolution 辛亥革命1911-1912 that ousted the Qing dynasty 清朝1644-1912. The scope grew well beyond this, and by the time of its publication in October 2015 it incorporated 15 essays on late Qing and Republican Era 中華民國1912-1949 history.

Across events from the Taiping Rebellion 太平天國1851-1864 on to the early years of the Cold War1947-1991, Qin examines: why China's confrontation with modernity played out so differently than Japan's; the factors that shifted how receptive nascent political movements and thinkers were to the influence of the West, Imperial Japan and Soviet Russia; a re-evaluation of the Republican government's economic performance drawing on comparisons with population shocks during periods of historical turmoil; and many more equally probing questions.

This is a kaleidoscopic account of a complex period, yet it remains coherent and fascinating throughout as Qin elucidates the failure of Xinhai and the New Culture Movement 新文化運動1915-c.1921 to live up to their promise, and ultimate co-option as tools for a renewed authoritarianism. Somewhat underlining this point, the book was banned within months of its release, but digital copies can still be found online.
